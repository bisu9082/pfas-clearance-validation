\section{Extraction from the ECOTOX Knowledgebase}

All toxicity data derive from the U.S. EPA ECOTOX Knowledgebase ASCII release
\texttt{ecotox\_\allowbreak ascii\_\allowbreak 09\_15\_2026}, obtained from
\sloppy\url{https://gaftp.epa.gov/ecotox/}\fussy. The release contains 725{,}636 test records
and 1{,}250{,}612 result records. We used it directly rather than through a
curated derivative, so that the number of records per substance --- the quantity
at issue in this paper --- reflects the primary compilation.

Selection proceeded in the order shown in Table~S1. Tests were restricted to
aquatic organisms in freshwater media; results were restricted to acute LC50,
EC50 and IC50 and chronic NOEC, LOEC, LOEL, NOEL and MATC endpoints reported in
mass-concentration units; and values carrying an inequality qualifier were
separated out. Records in molar units (\texttt{uM}, \texttt{nM},
\texttt{mM}, \texttt{nmol/L}, \texttt{umol/L}) and in dose or body-weight units
(\texttt{ppm diet}, \texttt{mg/kg bdwt}) were not converted and were excluded;
molar units alone account for 3{,}967 of the rejected PFAS records.

\section{Endpoint constructions and the effort diagnostic}

duplicate rows are removed, so the unit of analysis is the parent structure and
the unit of effort is the distinct record. Four summaries of the same records
were compared, all expressed as

\section{Equal-depth subsampling}

Every substance with at least $k$ records was subsampled to exactly $k$ records
without replacement, the minimum recomputed, and the correlation with the
original record count recalculated, over 400 draws per $k$.

The baseline matters. A substance is eligible for depth $k$ only if it has at
least $k$ records, so the eligible set shrinks and shifts toward the
well-studied substances as $k$ rises. Comparing the depth-equalised correlation
with the whole-set value therefore mixes a change of sample with the effect of
depth. We report both: the correlation over the eligible substances using
\emph{all} of their records (the baseline), and the correlation over the same
substances at depth $k$. Their difference is the depth effect.

\subsection*{Why the residual is not identified as reverse causation}

Subsampling removes the effect of sampling \emph{depth}. It cannot remove the
effect of sampling \emph{breadth}, because it draws from each substance's own
record pool and therefore preserves that substance's dispersion and assay mix
by construction. Breadth is strongly effort-dependent in these data:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering

substances with at least three records). Spearman correlation against
$\log_{10}$ record count.}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}

\midrule

within-substance range              & $+0.818$ & $8\times10^{-16}$ \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

A draw of $k$ records from a more dispersed distribution still has a lower
minimum, and subsampling leaves that channel intact. The residual
$\rho\approx-0.34$ is therefore a residual, not an identified causal quantity:
it is consistent with reverse causation, with effort-dependent assay breadth,
and with the compositional shift toward chronic endpoints, and this design
cannot separate them. Earlier versions of this analysis described the residual
as reverse causation; that description was not warranted and has been withdrawn.

\section{Consistent rebuild}

Training and external sets were rebuilt on the acute median. Model settings were
those of the published analysis and were not re-tuned: 2{,}048-bit ECFP4
fingerprints (Morgan radius 2), random forest with 300 trees,
\texttt{max\_features} $=\sqrt{p}$, seed 20260620. Broad cross-validation used
five repeats of shuffled five-fold splitting with 160 trees per fold. Conformal
validation used 40 repeats of the normalised split-conformal procedure given in
the main text, with marginal calibration on a random 80/20 split of the broad
set and PFAS-conditional calibration on disjoint halves of the PFAS set.

untested inventory inherit a further caveat: they are calibrated on the 58
\emph{measured} PFAS, which are by this paper's own argument the well-studied end
of the distribution, so exchangeability with the untested inventory is not
established and the nominal 90\% should be read as a construction rather than a
guarantee.

\section{Clearance across hazard thresholds}

Because the two endpoints differ in protectiveness, a single threshold does not
provide a comparable decision rule. On the 58 parent structures carrying both
summaries the acute median lies above the protective minimum by $+2.66$
$\log_{10}$ units in the mean and $+2.32$ in the median, and 93.1\% of measured
PFAS exceed 0.1 mg/L on the acute median against 39.7\% on the minimum.

A ``stringency-matched'' threshold is a construction and is not unique. Shifting
0.1 mg/L by the mean offset gives 46 mg/L, by the median offset 21 mg/L, and
matching instead on the fraction of measured PFAS above the cut-off (39.7\% lie
above 0.1 mg/L on the minimum, so the matching value is the 60.3rd percentile of
the acute median) gives 41 mg/L. Clearance is zero at all three, and at every threshold at or above 10
mg/L, so no conclusion in this paper depends on the choice. We also emphasise
that matching the fraction of substances flagged matches the \emph{selectivity}
of a screen, not the protection it delivers, and that none of these values is a
threshold any regulatory framework applies. Clearance was evaluated across a
continuum for that reason.

\section{Structure standardisation and resolution}

ECOTOX identifies substances by CAS number and carries no structures. CAS
numbers were converted to the dashed form and resolved to SMILES through the
PubChem PUG REST interface at the published rate limit of five requests per
second; 4{,}311 of 4{,}757 chemicals resolved (90.6\%), and the remainder were
dropped. Resolution failures are not random with respect to chemical class ---
mixtures, poorly defined substances and some organometallics resolve less often
--- and we were not able to characterise the resulting bias.

Two standardisation choices differ from common practice and are stated
explicitly because they change the data.

\emph{Desalting keeps the fluorine-bearing fragment, not the largest one.} The
usual rule retains the largest fragment by heavy-atom count. For
tetrabutylammonium perfluoro-1-butanesulfonate the tetrabutylammonium cation
(17 heavy atoms) exceeds the perfluorobutanesulfonate anion (13 heavy atoms), so
the largest-fragment rule discards the analyte and models the counterion. We
therefore retain the fluorinated fragment where one exists, falling back to the
largest fragment otherwise.

\emph{Fragments are charge-neutralised before canonicalisation.} Without this
step a free acid and its salts produce different canonical SMILES and are
counted as distinct structures. In the present ECOTOX extraction, 103 CAS
registrations collapse to 87 parent structures under desalting with
neutralisation. The difference is not cosmetic: ten registrations resolve to
per\-fluoro\-octane\-sulfonate or its close derivatives, and their separately assigned
protective values span 9.1 $\log_{10}$ units, from $-6.00$ for the potassium
salt to $+3.13$; without collapsing they enter the analysis ten times, at ten
different effective testing depths, and can fall on both sides of a calibration
split.

\emph{Standardisation precedes summarisation.} The order matters and an earlier
version of this work had it wrong: the per-substance table was formed from CAS
registrations and the structure rule applied only downstream, so the primary
effort analysis ran on 103 uncollapsed registrations rather than 87 parents. The
table is now built by pooling the records of every registration mapping to one
parent and computing the endpoint once on the pooled set. Correcting it
strengthens the central result --- the effort correlation moves from $-0.734$ on
the uncollapsed registrations to $-0.749$ on parent structures --- and we record
the correction rather than only the corrected number.

\emph{Exact duplicate rows are removed before counting effort.} The PFAS
extraction returns 15{,}991 rows, of which only 4{,}744 are distinct in test
identifier, endpoint type, reported concentration and unit; these arise from
multiple result records attached to one test. Because testing effort is measured
by counting records, duplicated rows inflate it, and unevenly. Deduplication
leaves the finding intact: $\rho=-0.749$ on distinct records and $-0.703$ when
effort is counted as distinct tests (2{,}182) instead.

\section{OECD subclass assignment and statistical power}

Substances were assigned to structure categories following the OECD (2021)
definition and the 2018 OECD/UNEP structure series. The definitional gate
requires at least one fully fluorinated, saturated methyl or methylene carbon
carrying no hydrogen, chlorine, bromine or iodine. Carbons that are fully
halogenated but not fully fluorinated (CHF$_2$, CClF$_2$), methine carbons
($>$CF--) alone, and unsaturated carbons ($=$CF$_2$) do not satisfy it.

Applying the gate to the 87 parent structures of the present extraction excluded
four: 1,1,2-tri\-chloro-1,2,2-tri\-fluoro\-ethane (non-fluorine halogens on the
candidate carbon), 2,3,4,5,6-penta\-fluoro\-aniline and penta\-fluoro\-benzaldehyde
(fluorine attached only to an aromatic ring), and di\-fluoro\-acetic acid, whose
CHF$_2$ carbon bears a hydrogen. Eighty-three substances pass.

Categories were assigned by parsing the molecule as C$_n$F$_{2n+1}$--X--R with
head-group identity taking precedence over incidental fluorination. Assignment
uses connectivity from fluorinated carbons rather than requiring a literal
CF$_2$ bonded to the head group, so that perfluoroalkyl ether acids (for example
HFPO-DA, whose $\alpha$-carbon is a methine) and perfluorinated cycloalkane
sulfonic acids are classified by head group rather than falling through to a
residual category.

\subsection*{Corrections to the classifier}

Four defects in the cascade used in an earlier version of this work have been
corrected, and we list them because each produced visibly wrong assignments.
(i) The carbonyl search walked one bond from a fluorinated carbon, so an
$\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated perfluoroalkenoic acid --- whose acid carbon sits two
bonds away across a $=$CH-- that bears hydrogen --- was invisible and the
molecule fell to the residual category; the walk now crosses one sp$^2$ carbon.
(ii) The carbonyl test did not distinguish an acid from a ketone, ester or acyl
halide, so perfluoro-2-methyl-3-pentanone and ethyl perfluoropentanoate were
assigned to the carboxylic-acid category; an acid now requires a second oxygen
bearing hydrogen or negative charge. (iii) Category 700 was assigned whenever
any aromatic ring was present and nothing else matched, which placed
tetraconazole --- fluorinated on an ether side chain, with a dichlorophenyl ring
bearing no fluorine --- in a ``side-chain fluorinated aromatic'' class; the
fluorinated carbon must now lie within two bonds of an aromatic atom. (iv) The
fluorotelomer SMARTS was satisfied by any C$_n$F$_{2n+1}$CH$_2$CH$_2$-- unit
with $n\ge2$, which classified fulvestrant, a steroidal antioestrogen carrying a
C$_2$F$_5$ terminus, as a telomerisation product; the floor is now $n\ge4$,
matching the 4:2, 6:2, 8:2 and 10:2 series of the 2018 OECD/UNEP categories. The
cost of (iv) is that 3:1 fluorotelomer alcohol is assigned by head group
instead; we accept that in exchange for excluding pharmaceuticals.

these sizes 80\% power detects only $|\rho|\ge0.71$ to $0.92$, so the point
estimates carry essentially no information about differential transfer.

\subsection*{Correction to a previously reported positive result}

An earlier version of this analysis reported perfluoroalkyl ether acids at
$\rho=+0.695$ (95\% CI $[+0.14,+0.93]$, $n=13$) as the one class excluding zero.
That analysis was computed on the superseded EnviroTox-derived external set
and on the protective endpoint, and does not survive
recomputation: in the present extraction the category carries three substances
with acute records and is not estimable. We record the correction here rather
than silently dropping it.

We also tested whether class-level transfer merely tracks class-level testing
effort, which would make the class analysis a third measurement of the confound
rather than a statement about chemistry. It does not: across the six estimable
classes the correlation between class rank correlation and class mean
$\log_{10}$ record count is $\rho=-0.49$ ($p=0.33$). The sign is the opposite of
what the confound hypothesis predicts and the estimate is far from significant
on six points, so we draw no positive conclusion from it either.

\subsection*{What would be required}

Resolving differential transfer at $\rho=0.3$ requires $n\approx85$ per class at
80\% power, or 129 with Bonferroni correction for six comparisons. Across six
categories that is roughly 510 to 780 measured PFAS, against the 87 parent
structures the public record currently supports.

\subsection*{A note on multiplicity}

No multiplicity correction is applied to the intervals reported anywhere in this
work; they are nominal 95\% intervals. This is immaterial for the effort
association ($p=7\times10^{-17}$) and for the pooled transfer estimate, and it
is stated here because the class table above reports six intervals and the
reader should not treat any of them as a corrected test. Under Bonferroni
correction for six comparisons no class interval would exclude zero either, so
the conclusion is unchanged.

\subsection*{Conformal procedure actually used}

Equation~(1) of the main text defines a normalised nonconformity score, and all
conformal results reported here and in the deposited code use it. No
leave-one-subclass-out conformal analysis is reported in this paper. An earlier
exploratory version computed one using unnormalised absolute residuals, which is
inconsistent with Equation~(1); that analysis has been withdrawn and no number
in this manuscript depends on it. We state this because the discrepancy existed
in a prior draft.

\section{Applicability domain: dependence on the distance metric}

The applicability domain is the mean Euclidean distance to the five nearest
training structures, with the threshold at the 95th percentile of the training
distribution. Euclidean distance on a binary fingerprint counts shared
\emph{absences} among the roughly 2{,}025 unset bits as evidence of similarity;
Tanimoto similarity does not, and is the conventional choice in this field.

The median maximum Tanimoto similarity of an untested PFAS to any of the broad
training structures is 0.27, and 86\% fall below 0.40. By any conventional
criterion the untested inventory lies largely outside the domain of a model
trained on non-PFAS chemicals, and the 90.9\% figure should be read as a
property of the Euclidean rule rather than as a statement that these structures
are well covered.

This does not affect the clearance conclusion. At any threshold at or above 10
mg/L the point prediction of the median in-domain structure is already on the
wrong side, so no restriction of the domain changes the zero. It does affect the
in-domain count, the median interval width and the 33.9\% figure at 0.1 mg/L,
which are correspondingly optimistic, and which we therefore do not present as
deployment-ready quantities.
