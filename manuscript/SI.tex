\section{Reproducible data lock}

All counts in the revised manuscript derive from the files under
\texttt{canonical\_results/} in the public repository
(\url{https://github.com/bisu9082/pfas-clearance-validation}). Canonical isomeric SMILES were generated with RDKit.
PFAS were removed from broad training by CAS match, PFASSTRUCTV6 canonical-structure
match, or direct measured-PFAS structure match. Identical desalted structures in the
broad data were represented once, using the minimum log toxicity across duplicate
registry rows. The inventory was independently deduplicated and measured PFAS were
removed before it was described as ``untested.''

\section{Model and conformal settings}

The default model used 2,048-bit ECFP4 fingerprints and a random forest with 300 trees,
\texttt{max\_features=sqrt}, and seed 20260620. Broad cross-validation used 160 trees per
fold, five folds, and five repeats. Conformal validation used 40 repeats. Broad marginal
calibration used a random 80/20 non-PFAS train/calibration split and evaluated the fixed
PFAS external set. PFAS-conditional validation used a fixed model trained on all non-PFAS
structures and disjoint 69/69 PFAS calibration/test halves. Repeated-split percentile
ranges are reported because test observations recur across repeats.

\section{Applicability domain and deployment}

The training leave-self-out five-neighbor threshold was 5.887 in Euclidean binary-ECFP4
space. Of 18,851 unique untested structures, 17,128 were in domain. Cross-fitted broad
marginal calibration produced median in-domain 90\% width 4.09 log units and classified
3,138 structures as clearable. Because this calibration achieved only 0.659 mean PFAS
coverage at nominal 0.90, that count is reported as an invalid-on-PFAS comparison, not a
decision output. Calibration on all 138 measured PFAS produced median in-domain width
10.12 log units and zero clearable structures.

Deployment conditional coverage requires exchangeability between measured and untested
PFAS. The present data cannot prove this assumption. Therefore, the inventory intervals
are an assumption-qualified extrapolation, while the repeated-split coverage results are
the empirical validation claim.

\section{Algorithm, applicability-domain, and calibration robustness}

The external negative transfer on the protective (most-sensitive) endpoint is not specific
to random forests. Re-fitting the same structure-locked non-PFAS training set with two
alternative learners on identical ECFP4 features reproduced the failure (Table~\ref{tab:alg}).

Finally, the zero-clearance result is stable to the calibration sample: under
leave-one-PFAS-out recalibration of the conditional 90\% quantile, the quantile varied with

138 leave-one-out recalibrations. The deployment conclusion is therefore not an artifact of
a single calibration draw.
