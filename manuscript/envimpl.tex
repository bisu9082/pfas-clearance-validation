\noindent\textbf{Environmental Implication}

\medskip
\noindent
PFAS are persistent, mobile and bioaccumulative contaminants, found in surface
water at ng/L to low \textmu g/L and regulated at 0.65 ng/L (EU environmental
quality standard, PFOS) and 4 ng/L (US EPA maximum contaminant level). Measured
aquatic toxicity exists for fewer than one hundred of over eight thousand
registered PFAS, so criteria for the rest must come from models. We show that
their training endpoint, the most sensitive reported effect concentration, is
confounded with testing effort by up to 4.9 log$_{10}$ units, and that the
confound propagates into any criterion derived from it. We supply a diagnostic
that detects it.
